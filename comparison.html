Who hosts AI infrastructure, who owns it, and what powers it?

Comparison charts: regions, companies and countries

Seven charts compare where data center electricity is used, how much more will be needed by 2030, who owns and uses AI computing capacity, and how clean the power is in the countries that host it. Figures 1 to 5 are the core comparison charts; Figures 6 and 7 are extra views.

How is data center electricity use split across the world?

One hundred squares represent all the electricity consumed by data centers worldwide in 2024. Each square is about 1% of the total, roughly 4.2 TWh.

Data. IEA, Energy and AI (Regional Data, total electricity consumption, 2024). The figure covers all data centers, not only AI, and includes cooling and facility overhead. IEA regions overlap, so Rest of Asia Pacific is Asia Pacific minus China, and Rest of the world is the remainder.

How much new electricity will data centers need by 2030?

The increase in yearly consumption between 2024 and 2030 in the IEA Base Case, for the same areas as Figure 1.

Data. IEA, Energy and AI (Regional Data). The Base Case is a scenario, not a forecast, and the IEA table is rounded to whole TWh.

Who owns AI computing capacity, and who uses it?

For each company, the power of the data centers it owns is shown next to the power of the data centers where it is listed as a user.

Data. Epoch AI, Frontier Data Centers, operating sites only. When a site has several users, its power is split equally among them. Epoch’s confidence labels (confident, likely, speculative) are kept, and capacity with an unknown owner or user is shown separately. The data is a selection of large sites, not a census.

How clean is the electricity where AI data centers are built?

Carbon intensity of the national grid, in grams of CO2 equivalent per kilowatt-hour, for the countries that host operating AI data centers. Countries are ordered by the power they host.

Data. Ember, Yearly Electricity Data (emissions intensity of total generation); Epoch AI for the ordering of countries. The values describe the national grid on average, not the supply of an individual data center. Countries with only planned sites are left out.

What powers the grids of the countries that host AI data centers?

Share of electricity generation by source in 2025, for the same countries and in the same order as Figure 4.

Data. Ember, Yearly Electricity Data. “Other” groups bioenergy, other fossil sources and other renewables. The mix refers to national generation, not to the power bought by individual data centers.

How is AI computing capacity spread across countries, owners and sites?

Nested circles: countries contain owners, and owners contain individual sites. The area of each circle is proportional to the site’s current power.

Data. Epoch AI, Frontier Data Centers, operating sites only. Sites without a known owner are grouped as “Unknown”.

Have the grids of host countries become cleaner since 2018?

The carbon intensity of each host country’s grid in 2018 and in 2025, joined by a line.

Data. Ember, Yearly Electricity Data. Changes within 5% are treated as no change. Countries are ordered by the AI power they host (Epoch AI).

Data: IEA, Epoch AI and Ember. See each chart for the exact dataset, year and limitations.
